About the role

Trade rules are not statistics. A tariff line, a rule of origin, a licensing condition — each is a legal object with a citation, an effective date, and a jurisdiction. DRONA holds those objects in a symbolic graph and reasons over them, then uses language models only where judgement is genuinely required. That division is the whole thesis of the company.

This role owns the symbolic half. You will design the representation for rules and their provenance, build the reasoner that walks them, and define what it means for an answer to be defensible — traceable to a named instrument, at a named version, on a named date. It is the most consequential engineering seat we have, and it is deliberately the first one we are filling.

What you’ll do

  • Design and own the rule representation — schema, versioning, effective-dating, and jurisdictional scoping.
  • Build the reasoner that resolves classification, origin and licensing questions across that graph.
  • Define the evidence contract: what every answer must carry to be auditable, and enforce it in code.
  • Set the boundary between deterministic resolution and model judgement, and make that boundary inspectable.
  • Build the evaluation harness — regression suites over known-answer cases, with failures blocking release.
  • Work directly with the founder on architecture, and with the policy analyst on what the rules actually mean.

What we look for

  • Five or more years building backend systems where correctness was the product, not a feature of it.
  • Real depth in at least one of: knowledge graphs, rule engines, constraint solvers, formal methods, or compilers.
  • Strong Python, and the judgement to know when a problem does not belong in Python.
  • A track record of designing data models that survived contact with messy real-world sources.
  • Comfort owning an unscoped problem and returning with a scoped one.

Nice to have

  • Exposure to customs, tariff schedules, trade agreements, or any similarly rule-dense regulatory domain.
  • Experience pairing symbolic systems with language models in production.
  • Open-source work we can read.
