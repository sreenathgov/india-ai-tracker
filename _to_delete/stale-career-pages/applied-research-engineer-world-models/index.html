About the role

A world model for trade has to hold more than documents. It has to represent goods, the routes they take, the instruments that govern them, and what changes downstream when any of those move. Getting that representation right is a research problem; keeping it honest is an evaluation problem.

You will work on both. This is applied research — the output is a system in production and a set of measurements we trust, not a paper. You will have unusual latitude on approach and unusually little tolerance for results that cannot be reproduced.

What you’ll do

  • Design representations for goods, routes, instruments and their downstream effects.
  • Build and maintain the evaluation suites that tell us whether a change helped — including the adversarial ones.
  • Run structured experiments on retrieval, grounding and reasoning, and write up what did not work as carefully as what did.
  • Turn the results that hold into production components, with the founding engineer.
  • Keep a public-facing thread of the research going through Kanan Labs publications.

What we look for

  • A research or applied-research background with shipped artefacts — models, systems, or benchmarks others use.
  • Fluency with modern language-model tooling, and scepticism about it in equal measure.
  • Genuine rigour on evaluation: you have caught your own results being wrong and said so.
  • Strong Python and the ability to write clearly about technical work.
  • Willingness to work on a domain — trade — rather than on a method.

Nice to have

  • Publications, or a body of writing we can read.
  • Experience with structured-knowledge grounding, retrieval evaluation, or causal representation.
  • Background in economics, logistics, or operations research.
