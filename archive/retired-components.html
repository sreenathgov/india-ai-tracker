Questions about TradeWatch.

01What is TradeWatch?

TradeWatch is Kanan Labs' agentic trade documentation and regulatory intelligence system for cross-border manufacturers.

It is being built to automate end-to-end trade documentation workflows across export documentation, evidence readiness, intermediary handoff, and shipment-level intelligence. The first focus is cross-border manufacturers in advanced manufacturing and EV supply chains.

TradeWatch is powered by DRONA, Kanan's proprietary regulatory intelligence engine.

02Who is TradeWatch built for?

TradeWatch is built for manufacturers and exporters operating across regulated cross-border value chains.

The initial focus is on EV and advanced manufacturing exporters, where shipment documentation often touches multiple domains at once: customs, marine cargo insurance, trade finance, product classification, regulatory evidence, and destination-market requirements.

03What workflows will TradeWatch support?

TradeWatch is being built to support three core trade documentation workflows:

  1. Shipping Bill readiness for CHA handoff.
  2. Marine cargo insurance broker packet preparation.
  3. Letter of Credit readiness and document consistency checks.

Alongside these workflows, TradeWatch will provide a shipment intelligence dashboard that helps teams understand document gaps, evidence risks, readiness status, and unresolved exceptions before submission.

04What does “end-to-end trade documentation automation” mean?

It means TradeWatch is designed to automate the work around trade documentation: extracting shipment data, checking consistency across documents, identifying missing evidence, preparing recipient-specific packets, surfacing exceptions, and supporting handoff to the right intermediary.

It does not mean TradeWatch replaces licensed actors. CHAs, brokers, banks, and authorised representatives remain responsible for actions that require statutory authority, regulated judgement, or official submission rights.

05How does the TradeWatch workflow work?

Customers share a shipment document bundle through the most suitable intake method for their size and operating model. For some customers, that may begin with email or assisted upload. For larger customers, it may evolve into structured folders, integrations, or workflow-based intake.

TradeWatch then reads the shipment bundle, extracts key fields, reconciles documents against each other, checks readiness against the relevant workflow, flags issues, and prepares structured outputs for review and handoff.

06What is the TradeWatch intelligence dashboard?

The intelligence dashboard is the operational view of a shipment's documentation posture.

It is designed to show what is ready, what is blocked, what is unclear, what evidence is missing, and which downstream workflow is affected. Over time, this also creates a structured view of recurring documentation patterns across shipments, corridors, products, and counterparties.

07How is TradeWatch different from a document automation tool?

Most document automation tools help teams fill templates or organise files.

TradeWatch is different because it applies regulatory and trade-document intelligence to the shipment itself. It is designed to understand which documents matter, which fields must align, what evidence is missing, why an exception matters, and which intermediary needs which packet.

The goal is not only to produce documents. The goal is to make the shipment's evidence position clearer before the packet reaches a CHA, broker, or bank.

08Does TradeWatch replace CHAs, insurance brokers, banks, or legal advisers?

No.

TradeWatch operates upstream of licensed and authorised intermediaries. It prepares, checks, organises, and explains the documentation position before handoff.

It does not file Shipping Bills, bind insurance, recommend insurers, issue legal opinions, submit documents to government systems, or replace regulated professional judgement.

09When will TradeWatch be available?

TradeWatch is scheduled for MVP release in early December.

Selected pilot customers can begin onboarding from July through a waitlist and pilot evaluation process. Pilot selection will depend on shipment profile, documentation complexity, operational fit, and the ability to test TradeWatch against real-world trade documentation workflows.

10How can a company join the pilot?

Companies can request early access and share basic information about their export profile, shipment workflows, documentation pain points, and current intermediary structure.

Kanan Labs will evaluate pilot suitability and contact selected companies with next steps. Pilot terms are available on request.

The Flow

A shipment becomes a fact. A fact meets the rules.
The rules produce a packet.

  1. I

    Documents

    Invoices, bills of lading, packing lists, certificates of origin. In any format your team already uses.

  2. II

    Reading

    Drona parses each document into a structured shipment fact model — bounded, source-located, replayable.

  3. III

    States

    Every obligation resolves to Open, Blocked, Conditional, or Unclear against the live rule pack for your jurisdiction.

  4. IV

    Review

    A human reviewer attests. The system records who, when, and on what evidence.

  5. V

    Packet

    Out: a hash-chained, source-stamped packet you can hand to a customs officer, an underwriter, or a banker — and defend three years from now.
