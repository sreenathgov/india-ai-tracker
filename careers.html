How we work

Three things that are not up for negotiation.

  1. I

    Every claim carries its citation.

    Nothing the system asserts is allowed to float free of the instrument it came from. If an answer cannot name its source, its version and its effective date, it is not an answer — it is a guess wearing a confident font.

  2. II

    Deterministic first, judgement second.

    Where a rule decides the matter, a rule engine decides the matter. Language models are reserved for the places where a human expert would also have to exercise judgement — and where they are used, the boundary is visible.

  3. III

    Direct challenge over quiet agreement.

    Disagreement is the cheapest thing we buy. We would rather have the argument in a design review than discover the flaw in a customs hall. Bring the objection early and bring it plainly.

What applying looks like

Short, specific, and no unanswered emails.

  1. 01

    You apply

    The form sits at the bottom of each role. Three written questions, your links, and a CV if you have one to hand. Half an hour, not an afternoon.

  2. 02

    We read it properly

    Every application is read by a person, and you get a reply either way within a week. The written answers carry more weight than the CV.

  3. 03

    Two conversations

    One about the work you have already done, one about the work here. Both are technical. Neither is a puzzle interview.

  4. 04

    A real problem

    Where the role calls for it, a paid exercise on something we are actually stuck on. You keep the work and we tell you what we thought of it.
